COMPETENCY 01
Research
To study how mobile threats work and use that knowledge to design and build an effective security solution called Mobile Defenders that protects Android users from real-world cyber attacks.

Mobile phones are used for banking, communication, and storing personal data, making them a major target for cybercriminals. To address this, I conducted research to understand how different types of mobile threats — such as malware, phishing links, ransomware, and fake apps — work and how they can be detected and stopped.

I started by studying existing research papers from international journals, including landmark works like Drebin, DL-Droid, and Andromaly, to understand what solutions already exist and where they fall short. This helped me identify that most existing tools depend on cloud processing, consume too many resources, and do not explain threats to users in simple language.

Based on this gap, I worked on designing Mobile Defenders — an on-device security app that uses both static analysis (checking app permissions and code) and dynamic analysis (monitoring real-time app behavior) to detect threats. I also designed the system architecture, detection logic, and auto-resolve feature that removes threats with a single tap.

The research was published in two papers in the International Journal of Creative Research Thoughts (IJCRT) — a UGC approved journal with an impact factor of 7.97.

COMPETENCY 02
Multi-Disciplinary
To use knowledge from subjects outside my main field of Computer Science — specifically Law and Management — and apply them in building Mobile Defenders to make it a more complete and useful app.

Building Mobile Defenders was not just about coding. I used knowledge from two completely different subjects — Law and Management — to make the app more useful and well-planned.

Through my Cyberlaw subject, I studied the Indian IT Act 2000 and its key provisions — Section 66 (unauthorized access), Section 66C (identity theft), Section 66D (phishing), and Section 43 (data theft). This legal knowledge was directly applied inside Mobile Defenders through a dedicated Cyber Safety and Legal Awareness screen, which informs users about their digital rights and the laws that protect them. This feature is not found in normal antivirus apps and it came directly from what I learned in Cyberlaw.

I also completed the IBM SkillsBuild course "Navigating the Project Management Lifecycle" on 06 November 2025. This course taught me how to plan a project properly, manage timelines, handle risks and deliver work in an organized way. I used these skills to plan the development of Mobile Defenders step by step — from research to design to coding to testing to final publication — making sure everything was completed on time.

Together, these two disciplines from Law and Management complemented my core CS knowledge to produce a more complete, legally grounded, and well-managed engineering solution.

COMPETENCY 03
Entrepreneurship
To understand how a technology project like Mobile Defenders can be turned into a real product that solves a market problem and creates value for society.

During the development of Mobile Defenders, I started thinking beyond just building the app. I asked questions like — Who needs this app? How will people find it? Can it become a real business? To answer these, I created a Business Model Canvas (BMC) for Mobile Defenders.

A Business Model Canvas is a simple tool used by startups and entrepreneurs to plan how a product can work as a real business. It helped me think about Mobile Defenders not just as a college project but as a product that real people can use and benefit from. I identified non-technical Android users — especially students, elderly people, and rural communities in India — as the primary customer segment, and designed a freemium revenue model offering free basic protection with paid advanced features like scheduled scans and PDF reports. I also planned B2B licensing to colleges and institutions as a future growth path.

I also completed the IBM SkillsBuild course on Navigating the Project Management Lifecycle which taught me how to plan and deliver a project step by step — just like a real product team works in a company. This exercise helped me understand product-market fit, go-to-market strategy, and sustainable business planning.

COMPETENCY 04
Multiculturalism
To understand how technology affects people from different cultures, backgrounds and abilities around the world, and to use that understanding to build more inclusive solutions.

As an engineer, I realized that technology does not work the same way for everyone. People from different countries, cultures and abilities experience technology very differently. I learned this through two courses that gave me a global and inclusive perspective.

I completed the Overview of Emerging Technologies course conducted by IUCEE — the Indo Universal Collaboration for Engineering Education — an international organization that connects engineers and educators across different countries. The course ran from August to October 2023 and included 6 webinars with assignments and projects, led by Dr. Shalini Gopalakrishnan, an Entrepreneur from USA. Learning from an international expert helped me understand how technology trends are viewed and adopted differently across the world. I learned that what works for users in the USA may not work for users in rural India — because of differences in internet access, digital literacy, language and culture.

I also completed the Microsoft certified course on Developing Keyboard-Accessible Products in March 2026, which taught me how to build digital products that are accessible to people with different abilities. This made me realize that inclusive engineering means building for everyone — not just the average user.

Both courses directly changed how I designed Mobile Defenders. The main screen has just one button — Start Scan — so even a first-time user knows exactly what to do. When a threat is found, the app shows a clear message in plain English with no technical jargon. The Auto Resolve feature removes threats with a single tap, and the Cyber Safety and Legal Awareness screen explains cybercrime laws in simple everyday language — so any user, regardless of their education or background, can understand their digital rights.

COMPETENCY 05
Social Consciousness
To build technology that helps society and to understand the responsibility engineers have towards the communities they serve.

As an engineering student, I believe that technology should not just be technically impressive — it should solve real problems that affect real people. My social consciousness grew through two experiences where I presented technology solutions to a wider audience and received recognition for the impact they could create.

I participated in the HITAM XPLORE Problem/Service Based Project Expo on 24th March 2023, where students present technology projects that solve real community problems. Participating in this expo made me realize how many different problems exist in society that technology can address. Seeing projects from students across different departments — all aimed at helping people — gave me a strong sense of what responsible engineering looks like. I secured 4th position in this competition, which validated that my work had genuine social relevance.

I also participated in the HITAM Project Expo Competition on 9th June 2023 and won 1st Prize. This was a significant moment because it showed that a solution built with the intention of helping ordinary people — not just technically skilled users — can be recognized and appreciated at an institutional level.

Both experiences were directly connected to Mobile Defenders. The app was built with one clear social mission — to protect ordinary people from cyber threats that they may not even know exist. Today, cybercrime affects everyone — students, elderly people, small business owners and rural communities — but most security tools are too complicated or too expensive for the average person to use. Mobile Defenders was built to change that.

The app helps society in three specific ways. First, it protects users from phishing attacks and online fraud — the most common ways people lose their money and personal data today. Second, it prevents unauthorized data theft by continuously monitoring the device for suspicious activity. Third, the Cyber Safety and Legal Awareness screen inside the app educates users about their digital rights and the laws that protect them — building long-term digital literacy in communities that need it most.
